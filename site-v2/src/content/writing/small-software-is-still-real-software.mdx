The software I use most often is rarely the most impressive software I have built.

It is usually a tiny thing with an audience I could fit around the kitchen table. It remembers a detail, removes a bit of friction, or turns a recurring family question into something we can see at a glance.

## A household problem is still a problem

HomeTeam began with the familiar pileup: calendars in different places, chores that existed mostly in someone’s memory, dinner plans in a text thread, and the daily chorus of “what are we doing tomorrow?”

None of this called for a startup. It called for a screen in the kitchen.

> The best feature was not clever. It was being there when someone needed it.

The first version was deliberately plain. Weather, meals, schedule, a few chores. Every addition had to earn the space it occupied.

## The useful constraints

Small software gives you a different optimization target:

- It can be specific instead of configurable.
- It can use the language your household actually uses.
- It can be finished enough without being finished forever.
- It can make one small part of the day noticeably better.

That is real software. Maybe the most honest kind.
